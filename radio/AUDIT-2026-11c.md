# The third outside audit, and the attack harness, 11 November 2026

An outside model reviewed the public radio branch again on 11 November 2026, after 0.9.11, and asked for tests in place of more reading. Each finding was checked against the source, then attacked for real: `node/src/bin/pentest.rs` starts a carry-all Lantern on this machine and turns malicious peers on it, printing PASS or FAIL with what it measured. AUDIT-2026-11b.md has the second audit.

Run it with `PYRATE_LAN_ANY=1 target/release/pentest <empty dir> <a wav> all`. One attack runs alone when named: `stems web slow flood cards deputy fuzz netfuzz loop`.

## The findings, checked

| # | Finding | Verdict | Done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | An accepted stem waits 20 to 90 s in a task holding its cargo, with no limit on how many wait. | Holds, and worse than suspected. Eight connections sending unique 3.75 MB stems took the target from 32 MB to 2.6 GB in 25 s: a phone would have been killed, a Lantern soon after. The cargo was not checked until after the wait. | Cargo is checked on arrival, before it is relayed or held: every entry parses, belongs to the station and carries a valid signature, the card verifies, every chunk matches its hash. Junk is refused at once and goes no further. Stems waiting to be shown are budgeted: 256 stems and 256 MiB on a Lantern, 32 and 32 MiB on a phone. The same flood, with cargo that passes every check, now peaks at 523 MB for attackers and target together, and a listener is served in 3 ms throughout. |
| 2 | A response is written with no deadline, so a reader that stops reading holds a serving slot. | Holds in the code. Not exploitable in the harness: 640 streams from 80 identities that never read left listeners served in 4 ms, because the per-connection and global limits hold. | Every answer now goes out within 20 s or its stream is reset, so a stalled reader frees its slot. |
| 3 | Beacons on the local network carry no signature. | Holds. Anyone on the same Wi-Fi could name another node at addresses of their choosing. | Beacons are signed by the node they name and dated, refused past two minutes, and an address is taken only if it is the address the beacon came from. | 4 | A fresh listener can take a revoked deputy's card, tip address included, before it has the station's log. | Holds. | 5 | A card's fields have no size limits. | Holds. ## Our own pass

The harness found what reading had not.

- **Carrier eclipse.** A station's carriers live in a list of 64, and the oldest went when the list filled. Node identities are free, so anyone could sign carrier claims from a thousand of them and keep pushing the real carriers out, and a new listener would find only nodes that never answer. A full list now takes a newcomer only in place of a carrier silent for ten minutes. A node this one trusts (a harbour, or one that has answered it) still takes the place of the stalest stranger. Carriers that keep saying they are there keep their places.
- **Half-open web requests.** 3,000 connections each sending half a request held nothing: the web listener served in under a millisecond, and again 40 s later. Nothing to fix; the attack stays in the harness.
- **Decoders and network fuzz.** 400,000 mutated frames through every decoder that reads a stranger's bytes, no panics. 3,000 mutated requests, then a listener served.
- **Loops.** A stranger's stem handed to two Lanterns that carry only their own stations is handled once.

## Results, 0.9.12

Every attack passes. The run is in SHIPS-LOG.

## What no test can close

- Two hops hide a broadcaster from any one node. Someone running both the guard and the exit can still match the two ends. The guard is kept for weeks so that draw is rare.
- Nine agreeing stations can move the network clock by at most two minutes, ten seconds a minute. Nine stations are not proof of nine people.
- A device that is unlocked and carries malware can be read whatever the app does.
- Pocket members see each other on the pocket's own topic.
