Start a Station

from this browser

Your stations in this browser are locked with a passphrase.

This copy of the player comes from a gateway that serves its files unchecked. To start a station, use a copy your browser checks or the app.

Give it a name, add some recordings, and it plays day and night. Nobody can take it down.

More options

A passphrase adds a lock to your 24 words. Someone who sees the words still cannot open the station. Forget the passphrase and the station is gone for good.

Bring back a station with its 24 words

How private is broadcasting from a browser?

Your station passes through other people's radios before it reaches anyone, so no listener can trace it to this tab. The relay this page connects through can see your address, though never what you send. Open this page in Tor Browser to hide your address from the relay too. The app on a phone is more private still.

Write these down

These 24 words are your station. With them you can bring it back on any device, or end it. They are shown once and saved nowhere, so write them on paper, in order. Nobody from Pyrate Radio or HOSTIS will ever ask for them.

    Keep your passphrase somewhere other than the words. Without it, the words open a different, empty station.

    Keep this tab open until your recordings finish sending. After that they play whether or not this tab is open.

    Talk to your listeners through this browser's microphone. They hear you about fifteen seconds behind, and your recordings carry on when you stop. Sirens, bells or voices in the background can give away where you are.

    Lock this station in this browser

    This browser keeps your station's key locked so a copy of its files gives nothing away. Add a passphrase for another lock, or have the tab forget the key when it closes. Your 24 words bring it back either way.

    Settings

    kept in this browser

    Each station says for itself whether it is mature or explicit. Hide any station that gets it wrong.

    Copy this list to share it, or paste in a list someone shared with you.

    Advanced

    Off, this browser always listens through two other radios, so the one with the station never sees you, and it waits when it cannot. On, it plays sooner on a quiet network, and the radio it asks can see this browser's relay.

    What this browser keeps

    Your settings and hidden stations, your name and mutes in the chat, any invitations you opened, and the key of any station you started here (never its 24 words). The key and invitations are locked under a key the browser keeps to itself. None of it is sent anywhere.

    Forgetting it all makes this browser new to the radio.
