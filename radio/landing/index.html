How It Works

from the key to the speaker
Every device is the network

Phones, computers, browser tabs and Lanterns pass the stations between them, and each checks what it gets against the station's own key.

Every device carries

Each holds a share of every station, about eight copies across the fleet, so a station plays on with nobody listening and its keeper offline.

Nobody sees who listens

You never give a name. Sound comes through two hops, and the count of who is aboard is made from tokens that change every hour.

Installing on stock Android

Pyrate Radio does not register with Google. From 2026 in some countries, and everywhere in 2027, Google's phones ask once before installing an app from a developer who keeps their name to themselves.

  1. Settings, About phone. Tap Build number seven times.
  2. Settings, System, Developer options. Allow apps from unverified developers.
  3. Wait the 24 hours Google asks for, once. After that it installs like any other app.

GrapheneOS, LineageOS and other de-Googled phones skip all of this, and so does an install over ADB.

Raise Colours

a station of your own

Name a station and drop recordings in. The clock plays them round the hour, the day and the week, at one loudness, as a loop, a schedule or a podcast's feed. Press the red button and you are live from the microphone, ten to twenty seconds behind.

The station is a key made on your own device and shown to you once as twenty-four words. Whoever holds the words is the station, and nobody can take it from them.

Light a Lantern

a node that never sleeps

A Lantern is the radio on a machine that stays on. It carries every station, relays for devices that cannot reach each other, and can put stations of its own on the air from folders of recordings. It opens no ports and works the same behind a VPN.

  1. Install Docker on any Linux machine, Raspberry Pi or server.
  2. Run these three lines.
    git clone --depth 1 --branch radio https://github.com/dukewilder/hostis
docker build -t pyrate-lantern -f hostis/radio/Dockerfile hostis/radio
docker run -d --name lantern --restart unless-stopped -v pyrate:/data pyrate-lantern
  3. It joins the network within a minute and carries from then on. docker logs lantern shows it working.

The Lantern guide covers stations from folders, a web page of your own, quotas and the open player.

Questions

asked and answered
Is it free?
Yes, and it stays free. Nothing is sold in it, to you or about you. A station can show a tip address, and a tip goes straight from your wallet to the station.
Do I need an account?
No. Open it and listen. A station is its key, and the key lives on the device that made it.
Does it work on iPhone?
Yes, in Safari, listening and broadcasting both. Open the open player and add it to your home screen to open it like an app. Listening with the screen off needs the app, on Android or a computer.
Who can see what I listen to?
No single device learns both who you are and what you hear. Your phone fetches sound through two other devices, the first knowing who asks and not what, the second what and not who. Counts are made from anonymous tokens that change every hour. What your phone carries for others sits on it sealed, and it cannot tell what it holds.
Can a station be taken down?
Not from any one place. Its log and its sound are copied across every device that carries it, and any one of them can hand it on. Only the holder of its key can end it.
Who decides what is on the air?
Whoever holds a station's key decides what it plays. You decide what reaches you. Hide a station and it leaves your dial. Explicit stations stay off it until you switch them on, and mature ones can be hidden too.
How much data, room and battery does it use?
Music streams at about 29 MB an hour and talk at about 11. The share your phone carries for others takes up to 4 GB, less on a fuller phone, and fills only on Wi-Fi while it charges, so away from the charger it costs you nothing.
Does my station need my phone to stay on?
No. Its programme lives in its signed log and every listener works out what is playing from the log and the clock. Put a song on a loop, uninstall the app, and the song plays on.
What happens if I lose my phone?
Put your station's twenty-four words into the app on another device and the station is yours again. The old phone can no longer speak for it. Without the words nobody can recover it, HOSTIS included.
How do people find my station?
It is on the Log and on the dial, at a frequency drawn from its key. Pass on its link, or print its poster, which carries a code that opens the station in any phone's browser.
Can I make a private station?
Yes, an invite-only station. It never shows on the Log. Its key is its invitation, and only whoever you hand it to can find it. Its listeners can see each other's devices, as in a private room.
Can listeners talk?
Each station has a chat for talk, cheers and requests the station can play next, and the radio saves none of it. A listener can also send the station a voice message only the station can open.
How do I know the app is genuine?
Check the file against the checksum under Take the set, and the signing certificate against the one the app shows. Updates arrive inside the app, signed by two of three HOSTIS release keys, and wait three days before they install unless you take them sooner.
Why “Pyrate”?
It is the spelling in Captain Charles Johnson's General History of the Pyrates of 1724, read on this radio around the clock. The old law called the pirate hostis humani generis, the enemy of all mankind. HOSTIS takes the name. The rest is at hostis.black.
