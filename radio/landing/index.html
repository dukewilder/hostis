The Promises

Nobody Can Switch It Off

There is no server

Phones, computers and Lanterns carry every station between them. Take hostis.black down and they all play on.

Only the key ends a station

A station with a hundred listeners lives in a hundred places, and the rest of the network keeps spares. Only the holder of its twenty-four words can end it.

It gets past blocks

Block the way in and the radios already aboard keep each other. Run it through Tor, over Wi-Fi with no internet, or pass a whole station as one file.

Listeners stay unseen

You listen without an account or a name, and nothing tracks you. Sound comes through two other radios. One knows who asks, the other what for.

Broadcasters stay unseen

A broadcast leaves sealed and travels about four radios before it surfaces. None of them can tie it to the keeper.

Carriers hold nothing readable

What a phone carries is sealed, so a seized phone shows nothing it carried. A Lantern runs with every port closed, behind a VPN if you like.

Nothing can be forged

Every recording is checked against its station's key before it plays. A carrier can pass a station on and cannot change a note of it.

Not even HOSTIS can stop it

HOSTIS can't remove a station, change what one plays or see who listens. An update needs two of three keys kept in separate places, and waits three days on every phone.

No gatekeeper

Anyone can start a station in a minute, free, and nobody approves it. The keeper decides what plays, and you decide what reaches you.

Installing on stock Android

Pyrate Radio does not register with Google. From 2026 in some countries, and everywhere in 2027, Google's phones ask once before installing an app from a developer who keeps their name to themselves.

  1. Settings, About phone. Tap Build number seven times.
  2. Settings, System, Developer options. Allow apps from unverified developers.
  3. Wait the 24 hours Google asks for, once. After that it installs like any other app.

GrapheneOS, LineageOS and other de-Googled phones skip all of this, and so does an install over ADB.

Three ways to carry

Carry the radio further

Listen

Every listener carries. The stations you hear pass from your radio to the next, so each listener makes the network harder to sink. Soon a Carry the radio switch lets you give it more.

Light a Lantern

The radio on any machine that stays on, like a Raspberry Pi or an old laptop. It carries every station and opens no ports.

The three lines
git clone --depth 1 --branch radio https://github.com/dukewilder/hostis
docker build -t pyrate-lantern -f hostis/radio/Dockerfile hostis/radio
docker run -d --name lantern --restart unless-stopped -v pyrate:/data pyrate-lantern

docker logs lantern shows it working.

The Lantern guide

Run a relay on a server

A browser cannot open ports, so every browser listener, iPhones included, reaches the network through a relay. Each new relay is another door that stays open when others are shut. One paste on a small rented server runs a relay and a Lantern together.

The one paste
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dukewilder/hostis/radio/radio/tools/vps/install.sh -o pyrate.sh && bash pyrate.sh

As root, on fresh Debian or Ubuntu. It asks for a name pointed at the server or makes a free one, lets in only what a relay needs, and prints your relay and Lantern at the end.

Questions

Is it free?
Yes, and it stays free. Nothing is sold in it, to you or about you. A station can show a tip address, and a tip goes straight from your wallet to the station.
Do I need an account?
No. Open it and listen. A station is its key, and the key lives on the device that made it.
Does it work on iPhone?
Yes, in Safari, listening and broadcasting both. Open the full player and add it to your home screen to open it like an app. Listening with the screen off needs the app, on Android or a computer.
Who decides what is on the air?
Whoever holds a station's key decides what it plays. You decide what reaches you. Hide a station and it leaves your dial. Explicit stations stay off it until you switch them on, and mature ones can be hidden too.
How much data, room and battery does it use?
Music streams at about 29 MB an hour and talk at about 11. The share your phone carries for others takes up to 4 GB, less on a fuller phone, and fills only on Wi-Fi while it charges, so away from the charger it costs you nothing.
How do I know the app is genuine?
Check the file against the checksum under Take the Set, and the signing certificate against the one the app shows. Updates arrive inside the app, signed by two of three HOSTIS release keys, and wait three days before they install unless you take them sooner.
