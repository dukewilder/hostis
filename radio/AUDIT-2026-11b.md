# The second outside audit, 11 November 2026

An outside model reviewed the public radio branch on 11 November 2026, after 0.9.8. Each finding was checked against the source before anything changed. This file records what held, what did not, and what was done. AUDIT-2026-11.md has the first audit.

## The findings, checked

| # | Finding | Verdict | Done |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A broadcaster's stem falls back to a plain `Hop::Stem` when the sealed two-hop push fails, so the node it reaches sees the station beside the sender. | Holds, in `stem_push` (hop.rs) and in the browser's `sail`. A resend also went plain. | Strict nodes and tabs send a station only sealed, two hops out, resends included. With no two-hop route the cargo waits and the station page says so. The plain stem stays only for an owner who allows direct connections. `privacy_test` checks it. |
| 2 | `dseal` returns the clear text when the browser cannot make its device key, and `keepSealed` stores it. | Holds. | Fails closed. With no device key, a secret is kept only for the tab's life (sessionStorage) and the studio says so. Nothing is written to lasting storage in the clear. |
| 3 | A pocket member's requests go straight to the pocket's hosts and members, who can see the member's device. | Holds, by design: a pocket member also joins the pocket's own gossip topic, where every member sees every other. Routing the log and sound through two hops would not hide a device already on the topic. | Pockets promise that no outsider can find, hear or tell one exists. They do not hide members from each other. The app, the open player and the how-it-works page now say so where an invitation is made or opened. |
| 4 | The network clock moves on five stations and up to five minutes. | Stale. 0.9.8 already needs nine stations, two-thirds agreement and at most two minutes. | Further: a correction now moves at most ten seconds a minute toward its target, so a sudden crowd of new stations can only nudge a clock while honest stations keep arriving to outvote them. A station's age is its own claim and cannot be trusted, so it gets no weight. |
| 5 | Joining chatter tells the room's peers this device listens to that station. | Holds, as an opt-in. Both the app and the open player already say so before the join button. | The notice is plainer and names the station. |
| 6 | An invitation handed to the open player on a gateway can be read by whatever script that gateway serves. | Holds for dweb.link, ipfs.io and hand-listed Lanterns, which serve files unchecked. inbrowser.link checks every file against its name in the visitor's own browser. | Invitations and flock links go only to the checked inbrowser.link addresses. The open player's studio says when it is running from an unchecked copy and points to a checked one before anyone keeps a station there. |
| 7 | A Lantern's hold key lies beside its data by default. | Holds. Already warned at start and movable with `PYRATE_HOLD_KEY`. | Moving it is now one setting: pointed at a missing file, the Lantern copies the old key there, checks the copy, and says when the old one can go. The Docker image keeps its default, because a key in an unnamed volume would be lost the first time the container is recreated, and with it the Lantern's own stations. dukewilder's Lanterns move their keys off the data disk. |
| 8 | Relay addresses in signed Harbour notes are taken without a policy. | Holds. A node could be pointed at loopback or private-network relays. | A relay must be http or https, under 256 characters, and name a public host. Loopback, private, link-local and `.local` names are refused unless the node itself runs on a test network of loopback relays. |
| 9 | The Lantern's podcast feed takes `X-Forwarded-Host` from anyone. | Holds. | `PYRATE_PUBLIC_URL` fixes the address outright. Without it the Lantern reads `Host`, and reads the forwarded headers only with `PYRATE_TRUST_PROXY=1`. |
| 10 | 512 requests at 4 MiB each could hold 2 GiB on a Lantern. | Holds as an upper bound. | Requests now draw on a shared byte budget as they arrive: 256 MiB on a Lantern, 48 MiB on a phone. A request that would pass it is dropped. |

## Our own pass

- **The browser had the same plain stem fallback as the phone** (finding 1). Fixed with it.
- **A tab that reloaded could lose its own station** when an exit with nothing to give answered first. Fixed in 0.9.9; the phone had the same fault and is fixed with it.
- **Speed between stations.** The logs of the stations either side of the one playing come aboard while it plays, in the app and the open player, so a swipe needs only its first chunk. `start_test` with a second station measures it: sound in 0.4 to 0.7 seconds after a swipe on a near network, and a warm open at 0.1 seconds to the dial and 0.8 to sound.
- **Finding a station from its 24 words** now keeps asking for forty seconds through fresh exits, in the app and the open player, where one empty answer used to end it.
- **The anonymity of two hops against many colluding nodes** is real and unmeasured. The how-it-works page already says two hops stop no one who runs both or watches both ends, and that Tor is the answer when safety rests on it. A route-selection simulation is the next audit's work.

## Not changed

- Public chunks are sealed against a carrier who does not know their address, not against one who does. That is the design: sealing stops a carrier from knowing what it holds, not a listener from opening what is public.
- The Lantern's plain web page and MP3 streams are ordinary web, and its page says the operator sees who listens there.
