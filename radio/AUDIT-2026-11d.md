# The fourth outside audit, 12 November 2026

An outside model reviewed the radio branch after 0.9.12 and sent seven findings with a test plan of some seventy items in eight areas. Each finding was attacked on the unfixed code first, fixed, and attacked again; a finding counts as closed only when the attack failed before the fix and passes after it. Our own pass found eight more, among them a crash any stranger could cause with one upload. This is the record for 0.9.13. AUDIT-2026-11c.md has the third audit.

## How to repeat it

- Attacks: `PYRATE_NULL_AUDIO=1 PYRATE_LOG_MB=64 PYRATE_LAN_ANY=1 target/release/pentest <empty dir> <a wav> <attack>`, built with `--features playback`. Attacks: `stems slow flood cards deputy fuzz netfuzz loop logflood cardwire logreply now routes nest web eclipse schedule`. The target runs in a process of its own, so memory and CPU figures are the target's alone. Run `schedule` last: it kills a target that does not answer in 90 s.
- Privacy: `PYRATE_NULL_AUDIO=1 PYRATE_LAN_ANY=1 target/release/privacy_test <empty dir> <a wav>`. `PYRATE_NULL_AUDIO=1` plays into nothing at the speaker's own pace, for machines with no speaker. Nothing chooses it on its own.
- Unit and property tests: `cargo test --release -p pyrate-core -p pyrate-node`. `core/tests/clock_fuzz.rs` takes about four minutes.
- Dependencies: `python3 tools/audit/osv.py Cargo.lock`.
- A baseline is built from a worktree of the commit before a fix, with its own `CARGO_TARGET_DIR`, and the current `pentest.rs` copied in.
- Test machine: Linux 6.18, 2 CPU cores, 8 GB, rustc 1.97.0. The audited base, db87162, includes 643d7aa and cbf6849.

## Each finding

**F1, carrier eclipse.** Path: `net.rs` `note_carrier`, `rank_carriers`; `lib.rs` `sync_log_direct`, `carriers`. Precondition: free node identities and gossip access; no stake. Impact: a new listener finds only carriers that never answer. A carrier's place is now earned: one that has served a valid entry or chunk in the last day, a neighbour, or a harbour door outranks every stranger and is never pushed out by one. Strangers that fail or fall quiet go first; otherwise a full list admits a newcomer by a random draw, one in four, so claims that arrive first cannot hold every place. A listener asks twelve ranked carriers four at a time, so fakes that hang cost one round, not a minute each. Tests: `a_false_claim_jumps_no_queue_and_a_flood_grows_nothing`, `claims_that_came_first_cannot_keep_a_real_carrier_out` (200 seeds). Parity: the browser uses the same `note_carrier` and `rank_carriers` with harbour trust. Limit: an attacker who serves real data earns trust like anyone, and then serves real data.

**F2, logs outside the quota.** Path: `store.rs` `put_entries`, `trim_logs`, `sweep_log_files`; `hop.rs` `fluff`. Precondition: a signature proves who wrote an entry, not that it is worth keeping, and keys are free. Impact: any phone or Lantern filled to its disk. Carried logs now share a budget (phone 128 MB, 64 MB a station; Lantern 1 GB, 256 MB a station; `PYRATE_LOG_MB` sets a Lantern's). A station over its share is taken no further for a week; over the total, the newest arrival nobody here has listened to in a day goes first, so a flood displaces itself. A stranger's entries are kept only for a station whose card, with its proof of work, is known. Own and kept stations are never counted. Parity: the browser holds at most eight logs of 32 MB each. Limit: a station live around the clock grows about 2.9 MB a day and passes a phone's per-station share in about three weeks; carrying it longer needs checkpoints in the protocol.

**F3, card tails.** Path: `net.rs` `Deserialize for Announce`, `verify`. A card is refused unread past 8 KiB on the wire or 512 bytes after its known fields. The harness sends the bytes raw, since the node's own reader now refuses to build such a card. Parity: the browser decodes cards with the same code.

**F4, browser limits.** Path: `browser/src/lib.rs` protocol handler. Four streams a peer and sixteen in all, a 64 KB request read within 10 s, the answer written within 10 s, paging loops that stop when a page brings nothing new, a 32 MB log cap. Test: `tools/test/browser_attack.py` opens the player headless against a private network (a relay Lantern with a station and a carry-all Lantern), tunes in, and runs `pentest --hit` at the tab through the relay while it watches the tab play; evidence in `tools/audit/evidence/browser-f4-0.9.12.txt` and `-0.9.13.txt`. A tab hands out no logs or sound, by design, so the probe asks for its chatter.

**F5, revoked deputy.** Path: `lib.rs` `stations_with`; `net.rs` `deputy_current`. Two co-hosts each hold their own appointment, so ranking cards by appointment would hide a live co-host. A deputy's card that this device cannot confirm against the station's log is shown as unconfirmed: the log's own name, about and art where it has them, the stricter of the two ratings, no tip and no cover. The app marks it UNCONFIRMED, the player "unconfirmed". With the revocation aboard, the card is refused. Invariant tested by `authority.rs`: no unverified deputy assertion becomes the station's word.

**F6, log replies.** Path: `archive.rs` `page`; `lib.rs` `Proto::accept`, `serve`; `pocket.rs`. Replies are pages of at most 2 MiB and 2,000 entries, and a shared budget (16 MiB on a phone, 128 MiB on a Lantern) is held from the request until the reply is written, so many askers wait instead of multiplying memory. Paging loops on both sides now run until the card's head.

**F7, web routes.** Path: `web.rs` `View`, `Views`. A view of each station (tracks, live tail, clock, feed) is built once per log length on a blocking thread, 128 at most; `/now` is worked out at most once a second per station. Limit: on this 2-core machine a listener on the cheapest routes waits 3 to 6 s while 64 local threads open 25,000 connections a second. The route answers in 4 to 8 ms; the wait is the kernel's accept queue. The Lantern's web port is not published through the tunnel.

**N1, nested bodies.** Path: `archive.rs` `framed::decode`. Bodies are decoded at most two deep in an entry; anything deeper is kept as unread bytes and is never valid, since a deputy may not write a deputy's entry. Test: `core/tests/nesting.rs` up to 200,000 deep.

**N2 and N3, rebuilt clocks.** Paths: `lib.rs` playout `run`, `now_for`, `OnNow`. The player keeps the tuned station's log and clock until the log grows; the Log page keeps a clock and names per station per log length.

**N5, costly schedules.** Path: `clock.rs` `Clock::new`, `walk`, `rota_now`, `deal`, `upcoming_n`, `now`; `archive.rs` `track_entry`, `latest`; `lib.rs` `schedule`, `provision`, `live_tail`. A station's schedule is computed by every listener, so whatever it costs, the station chooses. Each rota block's recordings are worked out once. When nothing is on the air the walk goes straight to the next minute that could find something (a recording added, a block edge, the bell), not a minute at a time. Answers are kept within a recording and minute. The bill's budget counts work, not steps. Titles, art and recordings are found by index. A station's colours, rota, programme, rating, details and art are found by index too. The schedule and the six-hour provisions walk the clock outside the store lock. Live tails read only the last 256 entries. Tests: `clock_fuzz.rs` (the costliest schedules and rotas a station can sign, a 20,000-name block, and three property tests that the quick clock and a clock that walks every minute and every track agree, on 250, 1,636 and 1,500 random logs). Parity: the browser uses the same core clock and indexes.

**N8, carried logs multiplied.** Path: `store.rs` `enforce_quota`, `missing_from`, `is_own_named`; `lib.rs` `mirror`, `serve`; `hop.rs` stem chunks. The log budget bounds what a Lantern carries, but four routines turned each chunk a log names into far more memory or disk lookups, all under the store lock; at the default 1 GB budget the sweep alone would have needed gigabytes. The sweep now returns at once when the hold is within its quota, and otherwise maps the files on disk and finds each one's station from the logs, so its memory follows the files held. The mirror reads each log 5,000 names a round from where it stopped. A stem's chunks are looked for from the log's end, 200,000 names at most. A chunk request checks an index of the node's own sound. Test: `over_quota_the_hold_lets_go_of_orphans_then_the_largest_carried_station_never_our_own`. Parity: the browser keeps no hold on disk and mirrors nothing.

**N6, torn logs.** Path: `store.rs` `read_sealed_len`, `open_log`, `put_entries`. A file is cut back to its last whole record when it opens, never when its first record fails to open (a wrong key). A write that fails part way is cut back and the log reloaded. A station's own entries are synced to disk before anyone hears of them. Test: `a_log_torn_by_a_crash_is_cut_back_and_keeps_what_comes_after`.

| Listening in a browser | That the player's host and a relay are used | As the app | As the app | As the app | Not used | The tab's address, always: tabs reach the network through a relay | Nothing | Everything in the tab |

## What no test can close

- Whoever runs both hops of a route, or watches both ends, can match them. A guard kept for weeks makes that draw rarer, not impossible.
- Many station keys or node keys are not proof of many people.
- An unlocked device with malware on it can be read whatever the app does.
- Members of an invite-only station see each other's devices.
- A station live around the clock outgrows a phone's share for one station in about three weeks.
- A station can still sign a schedule that costs its own listeners about 4% of a core while they play it (one-second recordings and a bell every minute). Nobody else's listeners pay for it.
