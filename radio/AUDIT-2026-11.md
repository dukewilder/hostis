# Security and anonymity audit, 11 November 2026

An outside static review of the radio branch raised sixteen findings. Each was checked
against the source at 66240bd, and a further pass looked for the same kinds of fault
elsewhere. This file records the verdict on each, what was done, and how it is tested.

## The rule the fixes follow

Privacy fails closed. When the radio cannot keep a promise it made to the person using it,
it waits, says so, and asks before doing anything that would show more. Nothing that names
a station, a listener or a keeper leaves a device by a weaker route without the owner having
turned that route on.

## The outside findings

| # | Finding | Verdict | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Private fetches fall to one hop when fewer than two nodes are known | Confirmed | One hop only with the direct switch on; otherwise the fetch waits |
| 2 | Strict mode does not cover station logs | Confirmed | Every request that names a station or chunk goes through one policy: logs, chunks, own-station sync |
| 3 | Broadcasters go direct after three failed stems; the stem's first hop sees the station | Confirmed, the second part partly (every node relays stems, so the first hop cannot tell an origin from a relay, but in a small network the odds are poor) | No direct broadcast without the switch, and the owner is told the station is waiting. The stem now starts two hops out: the guard forwards a sealed blob to a second node, which opens it and starts the stem, so the guard never sees which station |
| 4 | The browser fetches logs and sound straight from carriers | Confirmed (the relay hides the tab's address from carriers, but the relay sees which carriers the tab talks to) | The tab fetches logs and sound through two hops, as the app does, and goes direct only with its own switch on |
| 5 | Keys sit beside the data they seal on Lanterns and Linux | Confirmed for Lanterns and Linux computers; Android, Windows and macOS already wrap the hold key | node.key, the counting seed, a hand's key and the fetch guard are sealed under the hold key. Linux computers keep the hold key in the Secret Service keyring where one runs. A Lantern warns at start when hold.key lies beside its data. The counting key moves forward one way each day, so a key taken today cannot work out yesterday's tokens |
| 6 | The open player keeps a deputy key readable in localStorage | Confirmed | Secrets the tab keeps are sealed under a key the browser itself holds and will not hand to scripts (non-extractable, in IndexedDB) |
| 7 | Carrier notes are not signed by the node they name | Confirmed | A new signed carrier note, checked against the node's own key and time-limited. Unsigned notes are no longer believed |
| 8 | A chunk can claim 65,535 frames and make the decoder fill 240 MiB | Confirmed | Logs refuse chunks over 300 frames or 256 KiB; decoders stop at 300 frames whatever a chunk claims |
| 9 | The browser broadcasts its count token when the private route fails | Confirmed | The token waits for the next hour instead |
| 10 | Listening to a Lantern's web page or MP3 stream shows the listener to that Lantern | By design | Said plainly on the Lantern's page, in LANTERN.md and on the how-it-works page. The page now sends no referrer |
| 11 | Sealing is keyed by public fingerprints | Confirmed, as designed; the claim overreached | The copy now says a carrier can tell what it holds only by fetching the station's public log, and that the hold key is what keeps a seized disk unreadable |
| 12 | Five stations can move a device's clock | Confirmed | Nine stations, a majority of them within a few seconds of each other, and a two-minute ceiling |
| 13 | Pocket invitations and the token key sit readable in localStorage | Confirmed | Sealed as under 6; the tab's token key is replaced every 30 days |
| 14 | "Nobody keeps" a hail or chatter | Confirmed overclaim | The copy says what the radio keeps (nothing on disk) and that anyone in a room, or a keeper, can record what they hear |
| 15 | No limit on requests a peer can open at once | Confirmed | 48 requests at a time per node, 8 per connection, 4 MiB a request, 20 seconds to arrive |
| 16 | Computers and Lanterns log station and peer names | Confirmed | The app on a computer logs nothing unless PYRATE_LOG is set; a Lantern stays quiet with PYRATE_QUIET |

## Not changed, and why

- **Two hops are not Tor.** The radio's own routes keep each hop from learning both who and what, and nothing more. Through Tor stays the answer for anyone who needs more, and the pages say so.
- **Sybil and eclipse resistance** depends on how many independent people run nodes. Guards are kept for weeks and veterans are preferred, which narrows it; a simulation of hostile fleets is the next step, not done here.
- **Reproducible builds** are still to come. The Android certificate, checksums and the source are published, and `tools/apk_compare.py` compares a build with ours file by file.

## Tests

- `privacy_test` (new): a listener with private routes failing never sends a station-naming request directly, and with the switch on it does. A broadcaster's stem never goes direct without the switch.
- Unit tests for the chunk limits, the clock's quorum, the signed carrier note and the counting key's daily step.
- The earlier suites (`grid_test`, `share_test`, `start_test`, the library tests) still pass.
