Every book here was once banned or pursued, and every one is now in the public domain. LibriVox volunteers read them and give their recordings to the public domain too. The books play in order, a chapter at a time, round the clock.

Milton printed Areopagitica in 1644 without the licence it argued against. Common Sense came out in 1776 with no author's name on it. Candide was condemned in Geneva and Paris the year it appeared.

Douglass sailed for Britain in 1845, months after his Narrative named the men who had owned him. Thoreau spent a night in the Concord jail for refusing his poll tax. Emma Goldman was deported from the United States in 1919.

Ulysses was banned in the United States from 1921 to 1933. Lady Chatterley's Lover waited until 1959 there and 1960 in Britain, and a London court ordered The Well of Loneliness destroyed in 1928. Passages of Dorian Gray were read against Wilde in court.
